## Objective

Extend the finite mixed-unit mobility theorem from symmetric tensors to a
declared nonsymmetric mobility by separating dissipative and directional
parts, then state the exact Hilbert/form hypotheses needed in the proposed
large-dimensional setting.

## Frozen claim

After normalization, every real finite mobility has the unique split
`M=S+K`, with `S=S^T` and `K=-K^T`.  If `S` is PSD, only `S` contributes to
potential dissipation; `K` changes velocity with zero quadratic work.  A
coercive `S` plus a supplied PL inequality yields an unforced exponential
potential-gap rate.

## Claim ceiling

The finite exact apparatus does not verify an infinite-dimensional domain,
closed form, neural mobility, model-potential/physical-energy identity, PL
hypothesis, or biological forcing.

## Validation contract

Exact split, skew work, symmetric reduction, pure-skew motion, indefinite
failure, coercivity boundary, forcing work, PL rate, mixed-unit covariance,
dimensions, invalid exact inputs, dimensionless and adjacent regressions,
