Put $H_h=(I,h)$, $J_h=DH_h=(I,Dh)$,
$H_{2,h}=(0,D^2h)$, and $H_{3,h}=(0,D^3h)$. For
$S_h=Bh+g\circ H_h$, the third-order chain rule is

$$
D^3S_h=(B+D_yg)D^3h
+3D^2g[H_{2,h},J_h]+D^3g[J_h,J_h,J_h].
$$

Since the inverse affine base contributes three factors bounded by $\mu$,

$$
\Lambda_{3,\mathrm{out}}=\mu^3\{q\Lambda_3
+3K_2\Lambda_2(1+\kappa)+K_3(1+\kappa)^3\}.
$$

Thus $\Lambda_{3,\mathrm{out}}\le\Lambda_3$ preserves the C3 class.
For two graphs, with third-derivative distance $f$, expansion of each
multilinear slot gives

$$
f'\le\beta_3f+c_{32}e+c_{31}d+c_{30}\delta,
$$

$$
\beta_3=q\mu^3,\qquad c_{32}=3\mu^3K_2(1+\kappa),
$$

$$
c_{31}=3\mu^3\{K_2\Lambda_2+K_3(1+\kappa)^2\},
$$

$$
c_{30}=\mu^3\{K_2\Lambda_3
+3K_3\Lambda_2(1+\kappa)+K_4(1+\kappa)^3\}.
$$

Here $K_3$ also supplies the C2 predecessor's fiber modulus for $D^2g$.
The new $K_4$ term is unavoidable: comparing $D^3g(x,h_1)$ and
$D^3g(x,h_2)$ has no finite bound from $K_3$ alone. Together with the passing
C0/C1/C2 recurrences, strict $\beta_3<1$ makes the four-level nonnegative
triangular recurrence converge, hence the unique invariant graph is C3.

Strictness is necessary. At $q=1/8$, $\mu=2$, the C1 and C2 factors are
$1/4$ and $1/2$, but $\beta_3=1$. The local graphs
$h_c(x)=c|x|^3$ satisfy $h_c(x/2)=h_c(x)/8$ and are C2, while their one-sided
third derivatives at zero are $-6c$ and $6c$. Equality therefore cannot force
C3 regularity.
